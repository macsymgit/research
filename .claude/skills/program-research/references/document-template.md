# Document Template — Annotated 15-Section Skeleton

This is the canonical structure for a comprehensive program research report in this repo. Copy the skeleton, fill in the program-specific data, and follow the per-section guidance below.

## Document Header

```markdown
# [University] — [Program Name] ([Degree])
## Comprehensive Research Report: [Pathway Theme] | [Month Year]

> **Research date**: [Month Year] | **Live-source verification**: [Month Year] ([list of sources verified — UW program page, Job Bank NOC XXXXX wages updated YYYY-MM-DD + outlook, etc.]). | Sources: [list]. All figures in CAD unless noted. Hourly-to-annual conversions assume 40 hrs/wk × 52 wks/yr = 2,080 hrs.

---

## Table of Contents

1. [Executive Summary](#1-executive-summary)
2. [Program Overview](#2-program-overview)
... (15 sections)
```

The verification line is non-optional. Future readers (and future you) need to know when the numbers were last refreshed.

## Section 1 — Executive Summary

Four sub-sections, in this order:

### TL;DR for Parents
A single dense paragraph (5–8 sentences) covering: degree type, length, professional pathway, total cost (gross and net), expected entry-level and mid-career salary, and the single biggest financial or career risk. End with a line on what the modal outcome looks like.

### TL;DR for Students
A bulleted list (6–10 bullets), more tactical: workload reality, cohort size and demographic, key program-specific structural feature (e.g., mandatory year abroad, optional internship), what the licensing endpoint actually allows, and the most-misunderstood feature. Bold the load-bearing phrases.

### Core Numbers at a Glance
A two-column table (Metric | Value | Note) with ~20–25 rows. This is the single most-referenced part of the document. Every row should be filled; rows where data is not publicly disclosed should say so explicitly. See Section 14 (Quick Stats) for the full row list.

### The Three Things Most Applicants Get Wrong
Three numbered sub-sections, each ~150–250 words. These exist to balance the necessarily-high-level TL;DR with three honest correctives. Common patterns:
- "They confuse [program A] with [program B]" — clarify what each leads to
- "They underestimate the math/physics/workload rigor" — give a concrete example
- "They overestimate the salary ceiling" or "They overlook [structural advantage]" — quantify

## Section 2 — Program Overview

Sub-sections to include:
- **Faculty & Administration** — Department, faculty, formal degree title, total credits/units
- **What "[Program Name]" Actually Means at [University]** — A precise definition of the discipline, with a comparison table to the most-confused-with adjacent program (e.g., ArchE vs. Architecture; Life Sci vs. BHSc)
- **Program Size & Demographics** — Cohort size, women's enrollment if disclosed, retention rate if disclosed. Hedge explicitly when data is not public.
- **Class Size & Format** — Year 1 lectures, tutorials, upper-year classes, capstone size
- **N Distinguishing Structural Features** — 2–4 numbered features that make this program different from peers (e.g., "Mandatory co-op starting in Year 1," "Year 3 at Cambridge," "Common First Year and Year-2 sort"). Each ~200 words.
- **What Makes This Program Different From [closest peer]** — Comparison table

## Section 3 — Admission Requirements & Costs

Sub-sections:
- **Required Grade 12 Courses** — Table with course, requirement, posted minimum, competitive grade
- **Admission Average** — Table with type (posted minimum, competitive, top decile, acceptance rate). Always note whether the university publishes per-cycle averages.
- **Supplementary Application** — AIF / Casper / portfolio / interview, with weight and tactical advice
- **IB / AP Equivalents** — Table
- **International Applicants** — TOEFL/IELTS thresholds, international tuition, total 4–5 year international cost
- **Tuition** — Itemized table for domestic Ontario (tuition, ancillary, co-op fees, books). Cite the live fee schedule.
- **Residence Costs** — Table by residence type with the current academic year's figures
- **Total Cost (4 or 5 Years)** — Itemized year-by-year table summing to gross total
- **Net Cost After Work Earnings** — Worked example showing the math
- **OSAP and Funding** — Entrance scholarships, merit awards, special funding

## Section 4 — Professional Pathway

For engineering programs: **The P.Eng. Pathway** (PEO in Ontario)
For medical pathways: **The Medical School Pathway** (OMSAS, MCAT, CASPer, MMI)
For architecture: **The OAA Pathway** (CACB → IAP → ExAC → OAA)
For unregulated programs (3D animation, life sci with no professional school): replace this section with a portfolio / industry-entry pathway

Standard sub-sections:
- The 6-step (or N-step) flow diagram in ASCII
- Each step explained with cost, time, prerequisite
- Total HS-to-credential timeline table
- What the credential actually lets you do (the "regulatory moat")
- Ongoing maintenance costs

## Section 5 — Co-op / Internship Detail

If mandatory: emphasize sequencing, term-by-term wages, employer types, P.Eng. or licensing credit
If optional: emphasize the participation decision, what the no-internship path looks like, and the financial / résumé consequences of skipping

Always include:
- Stream / sequence diagram (year × term grid)
- Term-by-term hourly wage table
- Total earnings calculation with worked example (low-bound and high-bound hours)
- Major employers list (organized by sub-sector)
- Common job titles
- Tactical advice for first co-op / internship

## Section 6 — Curriculum

Sub-sections:
- Year-by-year arc (Year 1, Year 2, ... Year N) with course names and codes from the live progression sheet
- Capstone description with 4–6 example projects
- Specialization tracks (if any)
- Optional dual degrees / minors
- Software and tools table
- "Where the Curriculum Diverges From [closest peer]" comparison

## Section 7 — Graduate Employment, Salaries & Realistic Outcomes

Sub-sections:
- **Employment Rate** — Cite the KPI report or institutional source. Note the timeframe (6 months, 2 years).
- **Where Graduates Work — Sector Breakdown** — Estimated percentages, hedged ("triangulated from")
- **Geographic Concentration** — Where graduates land
- **Common Job Titles for New Graduates** — Bulleted list
- **Entry-Level Salaries (Ontario)** — Table with sources, including Job Bank floor and any Glassdoor / ZipRecruiter / industry data
- **Mid-Career and Senior Salaries** — Table with NOC median, 90th percentile, Toronto sub-median
- **Career-Stage Trajectory Table** — Year 0–2, 3–5, 6–10, 11–15, 16+
- **Realistic Probability Analysis** — Stage 1 (graduation), Stage 2 (first job), Stage 3 (licensure if applicable), Stage 4 (mid-career outcomes). Use estimated percentages with hedges.
- **Structural Risks** — Cycle exposure, geographic constraint, etc., with mitigation strategies

## Section 8 — Job Market & AI Analysis (next five years)

Sub-sections:
- **Current Demand in Canada** — NOC code statistics
- **Demand Drivers** — 3–5 numbered drivers (housing, infrastructure, retirement wave, net-zero mandates, etc.)
- **Headwinds** — 2–4 numbered headwinds
- **Industry Forecasts** — BuildForce / CAGBC / sector-specific data tables
- **AI and Automation Impact** — three sub-sub-sections:
  - What AI is doing now (with specific tools / platforms)
  - What AI cannot (yet) do — emphasizing the regulatory floor
  - The Anthropic Economic Index v4 group-level data
  - Net effect on careers — risk by career stage
- **What This Means for Applicants** — practical advice for AI-fluency in this discipline

## Section 9 — Alternative Career Pathways

Sub-sections:
- **Most Common Trajectories** table — 5–6 paths with credential, percentage estimate, and senior salary range
- **Detailed Alternative Pathways** — 8–12 lettered sub-sections (A, B, C, ...), each with:
  - Trajectory description
  - Entry path
  - Credentials
  - Salary range (entry / mid / senior)
  - Outlook
  - "When this makes sense" framing
- **Pathways That Are Not Realistic** — corrective for common applicant misconceptions
- **Quick-Reference Salary Comparison** — One large table covering all pathways
- **Failure-to-Launch Reality Check** — Honest section on what goes wrong and how to mitigate

## Section 10 — Year-by-Year Action Plan

Per year (Y1, Y2, ... Y4 or Y5):
- **Single Dominant Goal** — One sentence
- **High-Leverage Actions** — 5–8 numbered actions with concrete advice
- **Success Benchmark by End of Year** — Bullet list

Plus a **Post-Graduation** section for the licensing pipeline (years 1–4 of work).

## Section 11 — Success Tactics

Sub-sections (each ~3–6 bullets):
- GPA and Academic Tactics
- Co-op / Internship / Job Search Tactics
- Professional License Pathway Tactics
- Networking and Career Tactics
- AI Tooling Tactics
- Financial Tactics
- Mental Health & Sustainability
- The Honest Reality of Effort (a workload table)

## Section 12 — Financial Trajectory & ROI Analysis

Sub-sections:
- **The N-Year Investment** table (per-year cost breakdown)
- **Co-op / Internship Earnings Scenarios** — 2–4 scenarios from no-internship to high-end tech
- **Net N-Year Cost Summary** table
- **10-Year Income Trajectory (Three Scenarios)** — typical, high-end, alternative path
- **25-Year Lifetime Income Comparison** — table comparing this program to 5–7 other paths in the repo
- **Risk Factors Affecting ROI** — 4–6 numbered factors

## Section 13 — Decision Framework

Sub-sections:
- **The 12-Factor Decision Matrix** — 12 numbered questions, each with two or three answers and a recommendation
- **Decision Score** — How to interpret 8+, 5–7, <5 yes-answers
- **Strong Reasons to Choose** — Table
- **Honest Limitations** — Table
- **Who Should Study** — bulleted "strong fit" and "not the right fit" lists
- **Overall Verdict** — Italicized blockquote, 4–8 sentences

## Section 14 — Quick Stats Table

The full reference table. See SKILL.md for the row skeleton. Every row filled or marked "Not publicly disclosed."

## Section 15 — Sources

Grouped by category, with inline `[domain](URL)` formatting:
- University / Program Sources
- Accreditation & Licensing
- Salary Data & Job Market
- Industry Reports
- AI & Automation Impact
- Related Repository Documents

End with a footer line:

```
*Research compiled [Month Year]. Data points are from [year range] sources unless noted. Salary figures are in CAD unless stated otherwise. Hourly-to-annual conversions assume 40 hrs/wk × 52 wks/yr = 2,080 hrs (Job Bank does not publish annualized figures). All claims are linked to publicly available authoritative sources or, where no public source exists, hedged with explicit methodology notes.*
```
